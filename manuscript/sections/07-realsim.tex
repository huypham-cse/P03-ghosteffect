\textbf{Scope of the fitted-law experiment.} Calibration and test records in Table~\ref{tab:realsim} are simulated draws from the same fitted distribution.
The underlying emulator measurements found \RsimFitLateWrong/\RsimFitLateObs{} wrong late Element family reads and
\RsimFitCoWrong/\RsimFitCoN{} pairwise late cofailures.
These zero counts do not establish error-free readers on new screens.
The table instead tests certification decisions and their asking cost under the fitted law.
The label and lag budgets differ: additional simulated labels do not constitute additional emulator trials.
The budget analysis in Section~\ref{sec:design-probes} must be read alongside the asking rates, tail cases and tied-budget comparison.

\textbf{Measured-parameter simulation.} Table~\ref{tab:realsim} reruns every mode on profiles built from these measurements (\texttt{params.csv} records each value's source), with per-class lags
and $\bot$ as no observation. On \textit{Element} every certified mode stays within budget (CP upper \RsimElemJointPvHi\%): the certified wait reaches the measured maximum. The product mode asks \RsimElemProdAsk\%. Joint certification, which needs no cross-family
independence, \RsimElemJointAsk\% with \RsimBaseLabelN{} labels per class and outcome and \RsimFiveJointAsk\% with \RsimFiveLabelN{}. Keying by class matters little here: with a net-heavy lab mix the pooled wait still reaches the maximum. Simpler mechanisms also hold: Theorem~\ref{thm:twohop} alone (\RsimElemGtwoAsk\%
asks) and rules with a 10 or 30\,s wait (\RsimElemRulesAsk\% and \RsimElemRulesThirtyAsk\%), whereas trusting the app's status right after relaunch retries
\RsimElemAppPv\% (the lag-blind reads). Two departures break the budget. Passive labels, whose lag logs come from uninterrupted sends, give product \RsimPassProdPv\% and joint
\RsimPassJointPv\%. A tail of \RsimTail\% beyond 14\,s that the logs miss gives product and rules \RsimTailProdPv\% (joint holds only because it asks \RsimTailJointAsk\%), and if the logs contain it, every mode asks on every
interrupted Element send. On \textit{K-9} no family can certify, so every mode, like Theorem~\ref{thm:twohop}, asks on every interrupted send. Trusting K-9's status retries
\RsimKnineAppPv\% of committed mail and loses \RsimKnineAppLoss\% of workflows. Unvetted fixed-wait rules retry \RsimKnineRulesPv\% under the assumed K-9 queued-mail lag law.
With a \textit{Sent folder}, fault-injected labels show committed mail that never becomes visible. No wait certifies, so \sys asks.
Unvetted fixed-wait rules retry \RsimKsentRulesPv\% under the same assumed lag law. Passive labels lead joint certification to retry \RsimKsentPassJointPv\%.
The corresponding product result with 5{,}000 labels is \RsimKsentPassFiveProdPv\%. On real apps the decisive pieces are G2 classification, fault-injected labels, and asking when the GUI cannot reveal the outcome. Calibration adds a guarantee that follows
the labels.
