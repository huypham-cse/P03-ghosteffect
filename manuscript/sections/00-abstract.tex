\begin{abstract}
Mobile GUI agents send messages, mail and calendar entries by tapping through apps. If an interruption lands between a commit tap and its confirmation, the effect becomes a \emph{ghost}: it may have happened, and redoing it through the GUI creates a new request that nothing deduplicates. On Android emulators with self-hosted servers, K-9~Mail keeps accepted mail in a permanent Outbox error after a lost SMTP reply, while Element and DAVx5 resolve the same fault through their
own idempotent retries. \sys resolves ghost effects within a duplicate/loss budget $(\alpha,\beta)$ using a write-ahead ledger, accessibility-text evidence, a certified wait
for visibility lag, and asking the user when nothing certifies. Verdicts are calibrated on whole fault-injected effects, keyed by fault class. We recommend product certification when
its independence assumptions have a structural basis and joint certification otherwise. In an LLM-agent experiment on the emulator, AndroidWorld's M3A duplicated \AgentDupN{} of
\AgentValidN{} valid K-9 episodes without recovery; with \sys at the action boundary asking a simulated user instead, \AgentGuardDupN{} duplicate in \AgentGuardValidN{} remained. Held out on Element at $\alpha{=}\beta{=}\RealPlanAlphaPct\%$, the calibrated planner retried none of \RealElPlanCommittedN{} committed messages and all \RealElPlanRetryAbsent{} absent ones. Simulations with measured parameters show that fault-injected calibration and per-class lag anchors are required.
\end{abstract}
