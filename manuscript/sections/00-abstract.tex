\begin{abstract}
Mobile GUI agents send messages, mail and calendar entries by tapping through apps. If an interruption lands between a commit tap and its confirmation, the effect becomes a \emph{ghost}: it may have happened, and redoing it through the GUI creates a new request that nothing deduplicates. On Android~13 emulators with self-hosted servers we
observe this directly. After a lost SMTP reply, K-9~Mail keeps accepted mail in a permanent Outbox error state, while Element and DAVx5 resolve the same fault through their
own idempotent retries. \sys decides ghost effects against a duplicate/loss budget $(\alpha,\beta)$ with a write-ahead ledger, accessibility-text evidence, a certified wait
for visibility lag, and asking when nothing certifies. Verdicts are calibrated on whole fault-injected effects, keyed by fault class. We recommend product certification when
its independence assumptions have a structural basis and joint certification otherwise. In an LLM-agent experiment on the emulator, M3A duplicated \AgentDupN{} of
\AgentValidN{} valid K-9 episodes without recovery; at the action boundary, \sys reduced this to \AgentGuardDupN{} of \AgentGuardValidN{} while asking when K-9 could not
certify the outcome. Measured-parameter reruns ask on every interrupted K-9 send, and show why fault-injected calibration and a certified lag anchor are required.
\end{abstract}
