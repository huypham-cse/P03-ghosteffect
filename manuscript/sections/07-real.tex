\subsection{RQ7: Emulator measurements}
\label{sec:eval-real}
Campaign~2 on the testbed of \S\ref{sec:impl} ran \RealElemN{} Element~1.6.62~\cite{element_android} trials against Synapse~1.164.2~\cite{synapse}
(\RealElemNofaultN{} without fault, \RealElemRL{} with a lost reply, \RealElemAckedN{} acknowledged then killed), plus \RealTimeoutN{} with a withheld reply. It also ran K-9~Mail~24.0~\cite{k9mail}
against GreenMail~2.1.2~\cite{greenmail} without and with an IMAP Sent folder, with the nonce in the subject, which the recipient sees, on test accounts that opted in.
CalDAV ran on Etar, DAVx5 and Radicale, and a Synapse transaction-id sequence completes the set (Table~\ref{tab:real}).
Campaign~1 (\RealOneN{} trials, no screen check) agrees on which apps retry idempotently (Table~\ref{tab:realcone}, appendix). Eight Element attempts left no row. Six failed at trial start, before the send, behind a system ANR dialog of an unused accessibility service. Two had committed their
message (one stopped by the operator, one we cannot trace). Their lags are unknown, so the lag table covers the \RealElemN{} recorded trials. Four campaign-1 trials were discarded because Android had re-spawned
Element (in two, a queued message stayed unsent for 45\,s). Outside Element $n$ is small (from 2 to 4 per K-9 cell), so these are characterizations. Lags are upper bounds:
one read is a uiautomator dump of \RealDumpLo{} to \RealDumpHi\,s. The host's load average was \RealHostLoadLo{} to \RealHostLoadHi{} on \RealHostCores{} cores.

\para{Idempotent retries per app, and measured redo duplicates.} Element resolved every lost reply exactly once (\RealElemRL/\RealElemRL). In the \RealElemStd{} standard cells its own same-txnId retry, sent 20 to 100\,ms after the reset,
was acknowledged before the fault (``retry, then fault''). In the \RealElemSticky{} sticky cells the fault came first. Element then re-sent the same txnId (\RealElemStickyResend)
or, without re-sending, reconciled its local echo with the event \texttt{/sync} returned (\RealElemStickySync). With a withheld reply it showed ``Sent'' via that echo, re-sent
after a 70\,s timeout, and stayed exactly once (\RealTimeoutOnce/\RealTimeoutN). Synapse deduplicated a retry from the same device, also after a re-login with the same device id
or a restart. It did not deduplicate one from another device, nor one after we aged the row past 24\,h by shifting its timestamp. So its retries are idempotent within $H_{\mathrm{dedup}}{=}24$\,h on one device. DAVx5 re-sent to the same URL with \texttt{If-None-Match}, Radicale answered 412, and every event existed
once (\RealCalOnce/\RealCalN, \RealCalKill{} after \texttt{kill -9} or force-stop): idempotent by a client-chosen name. K-9 has no idempotent retry by design: once the final ``.'' is written, a failure is recorded as permanent and later send passes skip the
message, which stays in the Outbox (campaign~1's ``gone after sync'' came from a field never recorded). Campaign~2 recorded a tap on ``Sync account'' in
\RealKSyncTapped/\RealKRd{} lost-reply trials but on ``Send messages'' only in \RealKSendmsgsTapped. An audit without a Sent folder therefore logged every tap and waited
\RealAuditWindow\,s after one action, too short for K-9's 15-min poll. Of its \RealAuditAttemptsN{} attempts, \RealAuditN{} were valid, and the rest failed before their action. New DATA followed ``Sync account'' in \RealAuditSyncData/\RealAuditSyncN{} trials, ``Send messages'' in
\RealAuditSendmsgsData/\RealAuditSendmsgsN{} and no action in \RealAuditNoneData/\RealAuditNoneN. Recomposing sent a second copy with a new Message-ID in
\RealAuditRecomposeDup/\RealAuditRecomposeN. A Sent folder does not help: K-9 appends there only after a successful
send (from \RealKAppendLo{} to \RealKAppendHi\,s after ``Send''), so a committed mail whose reply was lost never appears (\RealKSentWrong/\RealKSentN). A scripted GUI redo duplicated every K-9 send it
was run on (\RealKRecDup/\RealKRecN, distinct Message-IDs, so no receiver dedup applies) and \RealCalRecDup/\RealCalRecN{} CalDAV events: the ghost-effect duplicate of
Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}a, measured with a scripted rather than an LLM agent.

\para{Evidence families and lag.} Element's first read was $\bot$ in \RealFirstBot/\RealElemN{} trials, mostly because a force-stop relaunch lands off the room (\RealFsFirstBot/\RealFsN); first observed reads
were wrong in \RealFirstWrong/\RealFirstObs, all lag-blind. Late reads were never wrong, never two families together (0/\RealCoN, CI from 0 to \RealLateHi\%). Campaign~1's five ``co-failures'' rendered
no contradicting state (\RealOneCoContra/\RealOneCoN), the digest showed the nonce in \RealOneCoDigest/\RealOneCoN, and screen identity was not logged, so we re-score them as
no-observation: they are not evidence against A2, which now rests on the model and VP-CONTROL~\cite{zheng2026vpcontrol}. Stale screens do occur. In \RealPilotStaleDigest{} of \RealPilotN{} pilot trials (not among the
\RealElemN), the room-list digest on a verified screen showed an older message while the server held the copy. In campaign~1, AndroidWorld's accessibility forwarder
returned a minutes-old tree after network toggles. Those runs were discarded and are not in the artifact. Element's send-state indicator appeared with its row in every trial and read ``Sent'' in all \RealElemSentPolls{} sightings: not a lag-free hop-1 view. Lag depends on the fault class
(Table~\ref{tab:real}b): a network cut adds nothing over the \RealLagNofaultMed\,s reader floor, \texttt{kill -9} gives a median of \RealLagKillMed\,s (at most \RealLagKillMax\,s), and force-stop \RealLagFsMed\,s,
mostly navigation. No recorded trial was censored (\RealCensored/\RealElemN), but follow-up windows differed by trial, so the \RsimTail\% bound from zero of \RealElemN{} is only a reference
for the lag-tail sensitivity rows, not a measured tail.
\texttt{am kill} after HOME was a no-op for Element (\RealAmkillNoopElem/\RealAmkillNoopElem) but killed K-9 (\RealAmkillKnine/\RealAmkillKnine). Under a SIGKILL of the
emulator's qemu process (the guest loses its page cache), the WAL ledger lost no acknowledged row with \texttt{synchronous=FULL} (\RealLedgerFullN{} trials). With \texttt{OFF} it lost
from \RealLedgerOffLostMin{} to \RealLedgerOffLostMax{} rows in \RealLedgerOffViol/\RealLedgerOffN{} trials, and \texttt{NORMAL} lost none only because each test transaction ran in its own process.
