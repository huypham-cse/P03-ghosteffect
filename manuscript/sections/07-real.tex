\subsection{Emulator measurements}
\label{sec:eval-real}
Campaign~2 on the testbed of \S\ref{sec:impl} ran \RealElemN{} Element~1.6.62~\cite{element_android} trials against Synapse~1.164.2~\cite{synapse}
(\RealElemNofaultN{} without fault, \RealElemRL{} with a lost reply, \RealElemAckedN{} acknowledged then killed), plus \RealTimeoutN{} with a withheld reply. It also ran K-9~Mail~24.0~\cite{k9mail}
against GreenMail~2.1.2~\cite{greenmail} without and with an IMAP Sent folder (\RealKnineNosentN{} and \RealKnineSentN{} trials), with the nonce in the subject, which the recipient sees, on test accounts that opted in.
CalDAV ran on Etar, DAVx5 and Radicale (\RealCalTrialN{} trials), and a Synapse transaction-id sequence completes the set (Table~\ref{tab:real}).
Campaign~1 (\RealOneN{} trials, no screen check) agrees on which apps retry idempotently (Table~\ref{tab:realcone}, appendix). Eight Element attempts left no row: six failed before the send behind a system ANR dialog, and two committed with unknown lags. Four campaign-1 trials were discarded because Android had re-spawned
Element. Outside Element $n$ is small (from 2 to 4 per K-9 cell), so these are characterizations. Lags are upper bounds:
one read is a uiautomator dump of \RealDumpLo{} to \RealDumpHi\,s. The host's load average was \RealHostLoadLo{} to \RealHostLoadHi{} on \RealHostCores{} cores.

\para{Idempotent retries per app, and measured redo duplicates.} Element resolved every lost reply exactly once (\RealElemRL/\RealElemRL). In \RealElemAckedFirst{} of them its own same-txnId retry, sent 20 to 100\,ms after the reset,
was acknowledged before the fault (``retry, then fault''). In the other \RealElemFaultFirst, all in sticky cells, the fault came first. Element then re-sent the same txnId (\RealElemFaultResend)
or, without re-sending, reconciled its local echo with the event \texttt{/sync} returned (\RealElemFaultSync). A withheld reply also stayed exactly once (\RealTimeoutOnce/\RealTimeoutN).
In the \RealTimeoutClean{} trials not queued behind an earlier hung request, Element showed ``Sent'' via that echo and re-sent after a 70\,s timeout. Synapse deduplicated a retry from the same device, also after a re-login with the same device id
or a restart. It did not deduplicate one from another device, nor one after we aged the row past 24\,h by shifting its timestamp. So its retries are idempotent within $H_{\mathrm{dedup}}{=}24$\,h on one device. DAVx5 re-sent to the same URL with \texttt{If-None-Match}, Radicale answered 412, and every event existed
once (\RealCalOnce/\RealCalN, \RealCalKill{} after \texttt{kill -9} or force-stop): idempotent by a client-chosen name. K-9 has no idempotent retry by design: once the final ``.'' is written, a failure is recorded as permanent and later send passes skip the
message, which stays in the Outbox (campaign~1's ``gone after sync'' came from a field never recorded). A follow-up audit logged every tap (\RealAuditN{} valid of \RealAuditAttemptsN{} attempts, \RealAuditWindow\,s each, shorter than K-9's 15-min poll). It saw no new DATA after
``Sync account'', ``Send messages'' or no action (\RealAuditSyncData/\RealAuditSyncN, \RealAuditSendmsgsData/\RealAuditSendmsgsN, \RealAuditNoneData/\RealAuditNoneN),
while recomposing sent a second copy with a new Message-ID in \RealAuditRecomposeDup/\RealAuditRecomposeN. A Sent folder does not help: K-9 appends there only after a successful
send (from \RealKAppendLo{} to \RealKAppendHi\,s after ``Send''), so a committed mail whose reply was lost never appears (\RealKSentWrong/\RealKSentN). A scripted GUI redo duplicated every K-9 send it
was run on (\RealKRecDup/\RealKRecN, distinct Message-IDs, so no receiver dedup applies) and \RealCalRecDup/\RealCalRecN{} CalDAV events: the ghost-effect duplicate of
Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}a, measured with a scripted rather than an LLM agent.

\para{Evidence families and lag.} Element's first read was $\bot$ in \RealFirstBot/\RealElemN{} trials, mostly because a force-stop relaunch lands off the room (\RealFsFirstBot/\RealFsN); first observed reads
were wrong in \RealFirstWrong/\RealFirstObs, all lag-blind. Late reads were never wrong, so no two families were wrong together (0 of the \RealCoObs{} trials with both read, CI from 0 to \RealCoHi\%). Campaign~1's five ``co-failures'' rendered
no contradicting state (\RealOneCoContra/\RealOneCoN), the digest showed the nonce in \RealOneCoDigest/\RealOneCoN, and screen identity was not logged, so we re-score them as
no-observation. They are not evidence against A2, which now rests on the model and on the common-mode study of~\cite{zheng2026vpcontrol}. Stale screens do occur. In \RealPilotStaleDigest{} of \RealPilotN{} pilot trials (not among the
\RealElemN), the room-list digest on a verified screen showed an older message while the server held the copy. In campaign~1, AndroidWorld's accessibility forwarder
returned a minutes-old tree after network toggles. Those runs were discarded and are not in the artifact. Element's send-state indicator appeared with its row in every trial and read ``Sent'' in all \RealElemSentPolls{} sightings: not a lag-free hop-1 view. Lag depends on the fault class
(Table~\ref{tab:real}b): a network cut adds nothing over the \RealLagNofaultMed\,s reader floor, \texttt{kill -9} gives a median of \RealLagKillMed\,s (at most \RealLagKillMax\,s), and force-stop \RealLagFsMed\,s,
mostly navigation. No recorded trial was censored (\RealCensored/\RealElemN), but follow-up windows differed by trial, so the \RsimTail\% bound from zero of \RealElemN{} is only a reference
for the lag-tail sensitivity rows, not a measured tail.
\texttt{am kill} after HOME was a no-op for Element (\RealAmkillNoopElem/\RealAmkillNoopElem) but killed K-9 (\RealAmkillKnine/\RealAmkillKnine). Under a SIGKILL of the
emulator's qemu process (the guest loses its page cache), the WAL ledger lost no acknowledged row with \texttt{synchronous=FULL} (\RealLedgerFullN{} trials). With \texttt{OFF} it lost
from \RealLedgerOffLostMin{} to \RealLedgerOffLostMax{} rows in \RealLedgerOffViol/\RealLedgerOffN{} trials, and \texttt{NORMAL} lost none only because each test transaction ran in its own process.

\para{The planner on a real app.} We also ran the GhostRecover planner itself on Etar, DAVx5 and Radicale, reading only Etar's screens: its search and agenda views form
the hop-1 family. No screen shows DAVx5's acknowledgement, so the planner can certify only absence and must ask about committed events. Product calibration on \RealPlanCalN{}
scripted fault-injected labels (\RealPlanCalOneN{} committed, \RealPlanCalZeroN{} absent, \RealPlanClassN{} fault classes, no read error) certified absence at
$\alpha{=}\beta{=}\RealPlanAlphaPct\%$ in every class. On \RealPlanTestN{} held-out effects, scheduled before the test, it retried \RealPlanRetryCommitted{} of \RealPlanCommittedN{}
committed events (one-sided 95\% CP upper bound \RealPlanRetryHi\%), retried all \RealPlanRetryAbsent{} absent ones on a certificate, asked about the committed ones, and left
\RealPlanDupN{} duplicates.
