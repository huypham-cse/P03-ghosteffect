An agent taps Send in K-9~Mail and the server's reply is lost. The server has accepted the mail, but K-9 keeps it in its Outbox with a permanent error
(Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}a). An agent that trusts the screen sends it again, and the recipient gets two copies.
We call such an effect, whose outcome the resumed agent cannot see, a \emph{ghost effect}.

Mobile GUI agents such as AutoDroid~\cite{wen2024autodroid}, AppAgent~\cite{zhang2025appagent}, Mobile-Agent~\cite{wang2024mobileagent,ye2025mobileagentv3},
UI-TARS~\cite{qin2025uitars} and AgentProg~\cite{tian2025agentprog} complete multi-step tasks in unmodified apps, and
AndroidWorld~\cite{rawles2025androidworld} rewards them for changing app state. Many of these changes are \emph{effects} (a message sent,
an event created, a transfer made) that should happen exactly once. Phones are hostile to that: the low-memory killer reclaims processes, Doze defers jobs, and links
drop~\cite{android_lmkd,android_doze,sentosa2025cellreplay}. AgentProg redoes the step~\cite{tian2025agentprog} and checkpoint-resume frameworks re-execute the
interrupted node~\cite{khan2026resume,langgraph_durable}, so a committed ghost effect is duplicated while the agent reports success.

Existing fixes for tool agents do not transfer to GUI effects.
LIMBO~\cite{li2026exactlyonce} finds that idempotency keys cut duplicates from 28\% to 4\% and that frontier models duplicate in 56 to 74\% of episodes when a
read-back cannot reveal the outcome. It also proves that no verification-only policy is exactly-once under late commits without a bound on in-flight time. A GUI effect
differs in two ways. The agent has no key: the app may attach its own request id, but a redo through the GUI creates a new request. And the only read-back is perceptual: a reader's
errors repeat when it re-reads a screen, and a \emph{look-alike} (last month's identical transfer) makes a screen consistent with both outcomes.

Our approach expresses exactly-once as a \emph{budget}: the probability $\alpha$ of retrying a committed effect and $\beta$ of abandoning an absent one, conditional on the hidden outcome.
Classical sequential analysis prices it and gives two floors, for weak evidence and for deciding before the effect is visible (\S\ref{sec:theory-main}). Evidence read as
accessibility text makes the budget reachable: the app's local outbox (hop~1), a content-carried nonce that turns look-alikes into text matches (Lemma~\ref{lem:nonce}), and a
digest of the history list. Calibration over whole effects makes the budget certifiable, either per family, which survives any dependence among a family's views
(Proposition~\ref{prop:family}), or jointly over the cascade's stopping region, which needs no independence across families (Proposition~\ref{prop:joint}). The lag gets a
certified share of the budget, and a \emph{two-hop decomposition} (Theorem~\ref{thm:twohop}) hands late commits to apps that retry until acknowledged against a server that
deduplicates within a horizon. A write-ahead \emph{effect ledger}, owned by a controller outside the agent, records each effect before the commit tap. The \alg planner
(Algorithm~\ref{alg:recover}) combines families until the budget is certified and otherwise asks the user, whom we model as slow and fallible (Figure~\ref{fig:teaser}b).

Our emulator measurements run \RealTwoN{} sends (faulted, plus controls) in three open-source apps on Android~13 emulators with self-hosted servers, read with a screen-validating
reader (\S\ref{sec:eval-real}). After a commit whose reply was lost, K-9~Mail never re-sent: by design it records a permanent error, keeps the mail in its Outbox, and its
optional Sent folder never shows it. A scripted agent that redoes the step duplicated \RealKRecDup/\RealKRecN{} K-9 sends (distinct Message-IDs) and \RealCalRecDup/\RealCalRecN{}
CalDAV events: these are measured ghost-effect duplicates. Element (Matrix) and DAVx5 (CalDAV) resolved the same fault exactly once by themselves (\RealElemRL/\RealElemRL{}
and \RealCalN/\RealCalN). Their own retry is therefore safe, on the same device and within the server's dedup horizon (24\,h for Synapse); an agent's redo is not.
Element's timeline row and room-list digest were never wrong together once the message was visible (0/\RealCoObs), and its post-restart visibility lag depends on the fault class
(median from \RealLagNetMed{} to \RealLagLmkMed\,s, first visibility seen in every trial).
Held out at $\alpha{=}\beta{=}\RealPlanAlphaPct\%$, the planner itself retried none of \RealElPlanCommittedN{} committed Element messages (CP upper bound \RealElPlanRetryHi\%) and all
\RealElPlanRetryAbsent{} absent ones. On Etar and DAVx5, whose acknowledgement no screen shows, it certified only absence.

We recommend product certification when independence across families has a structural basis and joint certification otherwise, using fault-injected labels keyed by fault class. With the measured parameters, joint certification retries no committed Element send (CP upper bound
\RsimFiveJointPvHi\%) and asks in \RsimFiveJointAsk\% of workflows with \RsimFiveLabelN{} labels and \CertLagBootN{} lag entries per class, and on every interrupted K-9 send.
Theorem~\ref{thm:twohop} alone asks in \RsimElemGtwoAsk\% of Element workflows, and passive labels or an unlogged lag tail break the budget. In a model-based simulation of \SimN{} workflows,
the product mode asks in \SimGhostAsk\%, about as often as a rule cascade that retries \SimRulesPv\% of committed effects against its \SimGhostPv\%, and joint asks in \SimJointAsk\% with 2{,}000 labels.
In the LLM-agent experiment, AndroidWorld's M3A resent and duplicated \AgentDupN{} of \AgentValidN{} valid K-9 episodes, while \sys asked in all \AgentGuardAskN{} guarded episodes and left \AgentGuardDupN{} duplicate,
after the simulated user's wrong answer. This study covers two agents, one model and one app.

\para{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,topsep=2pt,itemsep=1pt]
\item A budgeted model of GUI-effect recovery with a certified lag share and a fallible user, where classical sequential-analysis results apply (\S\ref{sec:model} to \S\ref{sec:theory-main}).
\item View-level certification calibrated on whole effects, in a product form (independence across families) and a joint form (none), with exact finite-sample bounds (\S\ref{sec:theory-corr}, \S\ref{sec:theory-cal}).
\item The content-carried nonce and the two-hop decomposition with a dedup horizon (\S\ref{sec:twohop}).
\item \sys's ledger, fault-injected calibration and planner, compared with a same-evidence rule cascade in simulation (\S\ref{sec:design} to \S\ref{sec:eval}).
\item Emulator measurements of three apps: idempotent retries and dedup horizon per app, a permanent misleading failed state, GUI-redo duplicates, per-class visibility lags, family errors,
  and held-out tests of the planner on Element and on Etar and DAVx5 (\S\ref{sec:eval-real}).
\item An LLM-agent experiment in which M3A duplicates committed K-9 mail after a lost reply and \sys asks instead (\S\ref{sec:eval-agent}).
\end{itemize}
