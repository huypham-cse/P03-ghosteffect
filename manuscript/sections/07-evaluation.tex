\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

We ask seven questions. \textbf{RQ1}: Do the guarantees hold, and what do they cost? \textbf{RQ2}: What happens when reads are correlated or misses persist?
\textbf{RQ3}: How does \sys compare with recovery strategies, including asking and a rule cascade over the same evidence? \textbf{RQ4}: Does it stay within budget when the world
departs from its model? \textbf{RQ5}: What do calibration population, cross-family dependence and calibration size cost? \textbf{RQ6}: What do its mechanisms contribute? \textbf{RQ7}: What do real apps on an emulator do, and how does each planner mode fare with their measured parameters?
Every number is exact computation, simulation, host or emulator measurement, or an LLM-agent experiment, inserted by a generator from the artifact's CSVs. The agent study runs on an emulator; physical-phone evidence is reported in the Discussion, while broader VLM coverage remains outside scope.

\subsection{Setup}
\label{sec:setup}
Model-based simulation. The simulator instantiates \S\ref{sec:model} with 8 app profiles that differ in G2, hop-1 observability, nonce field (private, counterparty-visible,
none), an enumerable history (for the digest), look-alike probability, local-only storage, and outbox durability. Each interrupted workflow has 1--4 effects. Faults (agent kill, app
kill, blackout, both) and interruption points follow \S\ref{sec:impl}. The default world departs from the planner's assumptions: nonce misses are persistent ($\eta{=}1\%$ hidden
field, plus 0.6\% per view). Hop-1 views err at 1--5\% each plus 0.5\% persistently, and the Monitor misreads 1\% of terminal states. Digest views miss 3\% each plus 2\% persistently.
VLM views err at $\epsilon_0{\sim}U[0.06,0.26]$, $\epsilon_1{\sim}U[0.04,0.20]$, with within-view correlation 0.6 and cross-view correlation 0.1 (single-screen error is a free
parameter~\cite{pan2024autonomous}). The user answers wrongly on 5\% of asks (10\% with a look-alike) and takes 60\,s. Calibration uses 2{,}000 whole effects per app and outcome, each read by every family. Lag tables have 20{,}000 times. $\delta=\alpha/(10K)$
with $K{=}11$ (product), 8 (source families) or 5 (joint). All policies see the same \SimN interrupted workflows (5 seeds $\times$ 2{,}400). We report Clopper--Pearson intervals and exact
McNemar tests against the product planner with Holm correction.

Baselines. Redo~\cite{tian2025agentprog}. Checkpoint-resume~\cite{langgraph_durable,khan2026resume}. Atomix-style adapters with one
read-back~\cite{mohammadi2026atomix}. Verify-before-retry~\cite{mansoor2026verified}. Ledger + key~\cite{li2026exactlyonce}. Four baselines that may ask. The re-read
SPRT of our earlier design. The same-evidence rules have \sys's ledger, hop~1 with the same fallible Monitor, and the same families read once per view. They use a fixed 10\,s wait.
An acknowledged entry or a nonce match means done. They retry only if every available family reads absent, otherwise they ask. A lenient variant acts on the first
unanimous family unless another contradicts it, and a rule family sweeps the wait (5, 10, 30, 60\,s) and the quorum (all families, all but the VLM, any two). The
Theorem~\ref{thm:twohop}-only baseline classifies each app's G2 status offline and never retries: a G2 app's acknowledged hop-1 entry means done, everything else is asked.
Metrics: dup and loss from any cause (\% of workflows, including wrong user answers). Escalations are the percentage of workflows. $P_{\mathrm{v}}$ is the fraction of committed effects the planner
itself retried, which is the guaranteed quantity~\eqref{eq:guarantee}. $L_{\mathrm{v}}$ is its loss-side analogue. We also report blocking recovery seconds.

\subsection{RQ1: Guarantees and their price}
\label{sec:eval-guar}
For an i.i.d.\ probe the exact error probabilities are within budget in \SprtAllWithin \SprtCells cells. The SPRT stays within \SprtGapMax reads of~\eqref{eq:lb}.
trusting a nominal ``92\% accurate'' claim violates the budget for \CalNomViol\% of probe types (appendix). Light-tailed lags certify $\alpha_{\mathrm{lag}}{=}10^{-3}$ in
\LagLogThree. A Pareto tail of shape $\kappa{=}1.1$ (our choice, after the heavy-tailed delays Li reports~\cite{li2026exactlyonce}) needs \LagParetoTwo{} at $10^{-2}$ and
\LagParetoThree{} at $10^{-3}$ (Table~\ref{tab:lag}).

\subsection{RQ2: Correlated reads and persistent misses}
\label{sec:eval-robust}
With errors correlated within a view, an SPRT that re-reads one view reaches \CorrNaiveOne\% duplicates at $\rho{=}1$, while reading each view once keeps it at most
\CorrViewWithinMax\% (Table~\ref{tab:corr}, appendix). Across views, a view-level SPRT that assumes independence fails (\CorrViewCrossTwo\% at $\rho{=}0.2$). The certified
family verdict stays at or below \CorrCertMaxHi\%, escalating once all views being wrong exceeds the budget.


\subsection{RQ3: Comparison with existing recovery}
\label{sec:eval-e2e}
Table~\ref{tab:planner} replays the same \SimN interrupted workflows under every policy. Redo and checkpoint-resume duplicate \SimRedoDup\% and \SimCkptDup\% of workflows.
A durable Atomix log removes the agent-kill duplicates of the in-memory port (\SimAtomixDup\% $\to$ \SimAtomixDurDup\%), but its single read loses \SimAtomixDurLoss\%. Ledger + key has
the lowest dup + loss among non-asking baselines, yet retries \SimKeyPv\% of committed effects (\ClassKeyGtwoPv\% on G2 apps, where its single read precedes the server commit).
Always-ask has $P_{\mathrm{v}}{=}0$ but \SimAskAllDup\% duplicates and \SimAskAllLoss\% losses from wrong answers. Nonce + ask escalates \SimNonceAskAsk\% yet retries \SimNonceAskPv\%,
because one family with persistent misses cannot certify 1\%. The re-read SPRT retries \SimVonePv\%, over budget. \sys retries \SimGhostPv\% (CP upper \SimGhostPvHi\%) and escalates \SimGhostAsk\% of interrupted workflows with product certification, and retries
\SimJointPv\% at \SimJointAsk\% escalations with joint certification (\SimSrcAsk\% with source families). Theorem~\ref{thm:twohop} alone retries nothing but escalates
\SimGtwoAsk\% of workflows, because it cannot clear any effect of a non-G2 app.

Same evidence, rules vs.\ certification. The default rule cascade escalates about as often as the product mode (\SimRulesAsk\%) with similar total errors
($p{=}\SimRulesP$ for duplicates), because wrong user answers dominate both, but retries \SimRulesPv\% of committed effects (CP upper \SimRulesPvHi\%), \SimRulesRatio$\times$
more. The lenient variant retries \SimRulesLenPv\% (over budget). Tuning the rules narrows the gap (Figure~\ref{fig:pareto}, top). At $P_{\mathrm{v}}\approx0.1\%$ the best of
\RuleN{} variants (\RuleBestThreeName) escalates \RuleBestThreeAsk\%, as many as the product mode run at $\alpha{=}0.03$. At $P_{\mathrm{v}}\approx0.5\%$ the product mode, run at a
nominal $\alpha{=}0.1$ (a 10\% guarantee, not 0.5\%), escalates \SweepTenAsk\% against \RuleBestTenAsk\% for the best rule, and joint certification \SweepJointTenAsk\% at
$P_{\mathrm{v}}{=}\SweepJointTenPv\%$. No rule goes below \RuleMinAsk\% escalations. At $\alpha{=}0.03$ joint certification escalates \SweepJointThreeAsk\% at \SweepJointThreePv\%,
more than the tuned rules. The default world satisfies A2 by construction, which favours the product mode. What rules lack is a guarantee that follows the labels: the default
rules exceed the budget in \StressRulesOver{} of the 14 stress rows of Table~\ref{tab:stress} and in \PopRulesOver{} of the \PopRows{} interrupted-population rows of Table~\ref{tab:pop}.
The K-9 fixed-wait rule results are simulations under an assumed queued-mail lag law. Vetted rules with matched calibration instead ask on every interrupted send (Table~\ref{tab:matched}). Nominal i.i.d.\ bounds escalate least but exceed the budget at every $\alpha\ge10^{-2}$.

Where escalations go, and what certifies. By app class, local apps escalate \ClassLocalAsk\%, G2 apps \ClassGtwoAsk\% and other apps with a hop-1 or digest family
\ClassNonGtwoAsk\%. The VLM-only app escalates \ClassVlmAsk\%. This follows from its calibrated bounds at the configured error rates, not from a theorem. Of interrupted effects, \WhyStruct\% escalate because no verdict could certify, \WhyWait\% because the wait exceeds $W_{\max}$, \WhyContra\% on contradictions, and
\WhyUncert\% because agreeing evidence fell short of $\tau$, the only slack a tighter analysis could recover (a lower bound is open). Among certified decisions, hop~1 enters \ShareHop\%, the nonce \ShareNonce\%, the digest \ShareDigest\%, and the VLM is needed in
\ShareVlm\%: the accessibility-text families certify, and the VLM is a weak tie-breaker.

Over-delivery and user. The product mode's $P_{\mathrm{v}}$ is \SimBudgetRatio$\times$ below the budget: the lag and calibration shares are barely used, and the certified
union bounds average \DecompBcp\% of the 0.5\% test share against \DecompBtrue\% with the true rates (CP slack \DecompCPratio$\times$). Tightening $\alpha$ from $10^{-1}$ to $10^{-3}$
raises its escalations from \SweepAskLo\% to \SweepAskHi\%. With a user wrong on 20\% of asks, always-ask reaches \UserAskAllTwenty\% dup + loss, the rules \UserRulesTwenty\%, and
the product mode \UserGhostTwenty\%.

\subsection{RQ4--RQ5: Stress, calibration population, dependence, size}
\label{sec:eval-stress}\label{sec:eval-pop}
Under generative models that depart from the planner's (Table~\ref{tab:stress}, appendix), the product mode stays within budget (CP upper at most \StressMaxHi\%) except where
calibration misses what deployment sees: heavy lag tails absent from the logs give $P_{\mathrm{v}}{=}\StressLag\%$ (CP upper \StressLagHi\%). Logged, it escalates \StressLagCalAsk\%,
and an undetected delayed recovery in Doze gives \StressDoze\% (detected, it escalates \StressDozeAsk\%). For a G2-classified app whose server does not deduplicate, duplicates occur (\StressNodedupDup\% of workflows): G2 is an app property. In an interrupted world (stale history after restarts, optimistic entries, longer lags), Table~\ref{tab:pop} reports the results.
passive labels bring the product mode to \PopIwPProd\% (CP upper \PopIwPProdHi\%) and joint to \PopIwPJoint\%, fault-injected labels to \PopIwFProd\% and \PopIwFJoint\%, while the
rules stay at \PopIwFRules\%: certification uses what the labels show. Under a 5\% common-mode stale history (violating A2) the product mode retries \PopCmCProd\%, source
families \PopCmCSrc\% and joint \PopCmCJoint\%. Their cost is labels: at 2{,}000 effects source and joint escalate \NcalESrcAsk\% and \NcalEJointAsk\% against \NcalEProdAsk\%,
at 5{,}000 \NcalFSrcAsk\% and \NcalFJointAsk\% (Table~\ref{tab:nsize}). Under version drift ($\kappa{=}10$) the product mode reaches \DriftTenPv\% (CP upper \DriftTenPvHi\%), and a
200-effect shift test keeps it at most \DriftTenTestPvHi\%.

\subsection{RQ6: Mechanisms}
\label{sec:eval-mech}
Without the nonce the product mode escalates \SimNoNonceAsk\%. Without hop~1 it escalates \SimNoHopAsk\%. Without the digest it escalates \SimNoDigestAsk\% (Table~\ref{tab:planner}). Nominal i.i.d.\
bounds escalate \SimNoCalAsk\% but retry \SimNoCalPv\%. Declaring when uncertified loses \SimNoAskLv\% of absent effects. Visible nonces cut escalations to \SimVisAsk\% but alter
\SimAltered\% of effects. A uniform budget split changes nothing significant ($p{=}\SimPUni$). A six-symbol nonce has $\epsilon_0\le\NonceEpsZero$, and giving every saga step the
full $\alpha$ yields duplicate probabilities up to \SagaNaiveSixteen\% at $k{=}16$ (Tables~\ref{tab:nonce},~\ref{tab:saga}, appendix).

\subsection{Planned device study}
\label{sec:device-plan}
The emulator study ran M3A and T3A with one model on K-9 (\S\ref{sec:eval-agent}); it measures agent duplicate behaviour, not only app behaviour. Broader device studies will extend this to phones, additional agents and models across 40 effectful tasks over the apps of
Table~\ref{tab:apps} (appendix) with the faults of \S\ref{sec:impl}. The present study does not claim VLM or physical-phone coverage beyond the measurements reported in the Discussion. Hypotheses: \textbf{H1} redo-based agents duplicate most committed interrupted effects
(a scripted redo did so on K-9 and CalDAV every time, Table~\ref{tab:real}). \textbf{H2} VLM errors across views are positively correlated. \textbf{H3} force-stop breaks G2 for
WorkManager-based outboxes are not supported for Element or DAVx5, which recovered after relaunch. \textbf{H4} \sys keeps $P_{\mathrm{v}}$ within budget and escalates at least
$3\times$ less than always-ask.
