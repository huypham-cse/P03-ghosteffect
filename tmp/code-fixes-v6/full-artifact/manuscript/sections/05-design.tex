\section{\sys Design}
\label{sec:design}

\sys is a recovery layer beneath an unmodified GUI agent. It intercepts the agent's actions, records effects before they commit, and after any interruption
decides each ambiguous effect with the rules of \S\ref{sec:theory}. Its parts are an effect classifier, the ledger, the nonce writer, the probe families with their
calibration store, and the \alg planner with its saga layer (Figure~\ref{fig:arch}).

\subsection{Where it runs, and the ledger}
\label{sec:design-ledger}
\para{Topology.} The ledger must outlive the agent. In the host topology (our testbed), the controller is a host process separate from the agent, which drives the
emulator over adb; killing the agent then cannot erase the ledger, by process separation alone. In the on-device topology, the controller is an Android foreground service
in its own process, with the ledger in app-private storage. A foreground service has an elevated OOM priority but is not immune to the low-memory killer; safety does not depend on
it surviving, because the write-ahead invariant below holds across its death, and the controller resolves outstanding records when it restarts (sticky service or next agent launch).
We have built the host topology's decision core and ledger; the on-device service is design only.

\para{Commit points.} Agents with structured plans label effects directly~\cite{tian2025agentprog}; otherwise a conservative classifier matches the target's text, content
description or resource id against a per-locale lexicon (send, pay, save, post, book, confirm, delete), or a VLM flags it~\cite{lee2025verisafe,sun2025ossentinel}. A false
positive costs one ledger write; a false negative falls back to the agent's native recovery.

\para{Write-ahead ledger.} Each effect has a record \{\textit{id}, workflow, step, app package and version, description, nonce, history digest, attempt, state, $t_0$\}. States are
\textsf{PLANNED} $\to$ \textsf{ISSUED} $\to$ \{\textsf{COMMITTED}, \textsf{ABSENT}, \textsf{PENDING}, \textsf{ASKED}\}, with \textsf{ABSENT} $\to$ \textsf{ISSUED} on retry
(attempt$+1$, same nonce) and \textsf{COMMITTED} $\to$ \textsf{COMPENSATING} $\to$ \textsf{COMPENSATED} when a duplicate is detected. The invariant is write-ahead:
\textsf{ISSUED} is committed in SQLite (WAL mode, \texttt{synchronous=FULL}) \emph{before} the commit tap is injected. On restart, every \textsf{ISSUED} record is a ghost-effect
candidate and is resolved before the agent continues. This is the prepare--dispatch--settle discipline of Agent libOS~\cite{zhang2026agentlibos}, except that settlement is a
statistical decision.

\subsection{Content-carried nonce}
\label{sec:design-nonce}
A per-app field map names a free-text field that the app stores and displays: a private note on an event or contact, a memo on a transfer, a trailing tag on a message.
Before the commit tap, \sys types \texttt{gx:} and six Crockford base-32 symbols (30 bits; the alphabet omits I, L, O, U) into that field, and a retry reuses the tag.
The nonce probe reads the app's sent/history view through the accessibility tree and through in-app search, matches outgoing items of the verified account within edit distance~1,
and ignores drafts, failed entries and notifications (Lemma~\ref{lem:nonce}). With $M{=}40$ visible tokens this gives $\epsilon_0\le\NonceEpsZero$ per view (Table~\ref{tab:nonce}).
By default \sys uses only fields that the counterparty cannot see. Counterparty-visible fields (an SMS or chat body, a payment memo printed on a statement) need per-app opt-in, change
the content of the effect, and break graders that compare content exactly, such as AndroidWorld's SMS and Markor checks, unless they strip \texttt{gx:} tags.

\subsection{Probe families and calibration}
\label{sec:design-probes}
Each probe is a short navigation script plus a reader, grouped into families:
(1)~\emph{hop-1}: count outbox and sent-list entries created after $t_0$ that match the ledger description or nonce (two views: outbox list, conversation status);
(2)~\emph{nonce}: two views as above;
(3)~\emph{digest}: before the tap the ledger stores a digest of matching history items; after recovery the probe counts matching items in the history list and in search results
and reports whether a new one appeared. This answers look-alikes without a nonce, since last month's transfer is already in the digest;
(4)~\emph{VLM}: a yes/no question about the ledger description, asked once each of the list, detail and search views. The VLM family is skipped when the digest shows a look-alike.
Hop~1 reads the app's local database, and an app whose status is drawn only with the rendered history (Element) has no hop-1 family; the other three read the history as
the app renders it, and in-app search often queries the server. They are therefore one
\emph{evidence source}, and a stale history after a restart can fool all three at once. A server-backed search view inherits the hop-2 lag, so it counts toward ``absent'' only after the wait.

\para{Calibration labels.} For each (app version, family, verdict level or region) the store holds one-sided Clopper--Pearson bounds (Lemma~\ref{lem:calib}(ii)) from calibration
\emph{effects}, not reads. Lemma~\ref{lem:calib} needs effects drawn from the \emph{interrupted} population, so labels come from fault-injected runs on test accounts or a staging backend:
the controller injects the fault classes of \S\ref{sec:impl} at the commit point, records the true outcome from the test backend, runs every family once per view on recovery, and logs the
time from $t_0$ to visibility. Bounds and lag tables are keyed by the observable fault class (Lemma~\ref{lem:calib}(ii)), so the lab's fault mix need not match deployment's;
an effect whose class the controller cannot tell uses the per-class maximum. Passive labels (confirmed sends, abandoned forms) are cheaper but miss cached history after a
kill, optimistic entries and restart lags (\S\ref{sec:eval-pop},~\S\ref{sec:eval-real}). A label costs a fault-injected effect plus one read of every view ($\approx$15\,s), on test
devices.

\para{How many labels.} Validity holds at any sample size; small samples only widen the bounds and raise escalations (\S\ref{sec:eval-pop}). Table~\ref{tab:nsize} gives the sizes that
make certification \emph{possible}. Product certification needs a few hundred effects per stratum at $\alpha{=}1\%$, because per-family bounds multiply. Joint certification and the waits
need about $\ln(1/\delta)/\alpha_{\mathrm{test}}$ samples even with zero events, i.e.\ thousands at $\alpha{=}1\%$ and tens of thousands at $10^{-3}$, about twice that if the region's true
rate is a quarter of $\alpha_{\mathrm{test}}$, and four times that when keyed by fault class. A single user produces nowhere near this per app version, so the store is
pooled: lag tables per app and backend across versions (lag is a property of the network path and the app's sync, not its UI), and verdict bounds per app across versions and devices,
with a shift test on a small sample of the new version that falls back to that sample's own bounds (more escalations, not fewer guarantees) when it detects an increase.
The shift test is per family and level; pooling \emph{joint} regions across versions needs a region-level test, which we have not built. The nonce's
false-match bound is analytic. Pooling across devices assumes the devices are exchangeable within a stratum (app version, locale, VLM); per-device drift is not detected yet.

\subsection{The \alg planner}
\label{sec:design-planner}

\begin{algorithm}[t]
\caption{\alg: resolve one ghost effect $e$}
\label{alg:recover}
\small
\begin{algorithmic}[1]
\Require record $e$ (\textsf{ISSUED} at $t_0$); budget $(\alpha_e,\beta_e)$ from the saga layer; calibrated bounds $q$; lag tables
\State $\alpha_t,\alpha_l,\beta_t\gets 0.5\alpha_e,\,0.4\alpha_e,\,0.9\beta_e$ \Comment{$\alpha_{\mathrm{cal}}$ via $\delta$}
\State $\tau_a,\tau_c\gets$ largest thresholds certified for $\alpha_t,\beta_t$ \Comment{Prop.~\ref{prop:family} or \ref{prop:joint}}
\State \Call{VerifyForeground}{$e$.app}; $P\gets1$; $d\gets\bot$; $\ell\gets\alpha_l$
\If{$e$.app has a hop-1 family}
  \State $\ell\gets\alpha_l/2$; $(s,r)\gets$ \Call{Readout}{hop-1} \Comment{entries after $t_0$}
  \If{$s=$ com $\wedge$ (G2 $\vee$ local)} $P\gets q^{\mathrm{com}}_{\text{hop1}}(r)$; $d\gets$ com
    \State $o\gets$ acked; \textbf{if} G2: $o\gets$ \Call{Monitor}{$e$} \Comment{bkgd., fallible}
    \State \textbf{if} $o=$ acked $\wedge\,P\le\tau_c$: \Return \Call{Mark}{$e$, \textsf{COMMITTED}}
    \State \textbf{if} $o\in\{$failed, stuck$\}$: $P\gets1$; $d\gets\bot$ \Comment{hand over}
  \ElsIf{$s=$ abs} $P\gets q^{\mathrm{abs}}_{\text{hop1}}(r)$; $d\gets$ abs; \textbf{if} $P\le\tau_a$ \Return \Call{Retry}{$e$}
  \EndIf
\EndIf
\If{$d\ne$ com \textbf{and} app not local}
  \State $w\gets$ \Call{CertifiedWait}{$e$.app, $e$.class, $d{=}$abs ? $\ell/q_h$ : $\ell$} \Comment{$q_h{=}\max_r q^{\mathrm{abs}}_{\text{hop1}}(r)$}
  \State \textbf{if} $w-\text{elapsed}>W_{\max}$ \Return \Call{Ask}{$e$}; \Call{WaitUntil}{$t_0+w$}
\EndIf
\For{$f\in$ [nonce, digest, VLM] available for $e$}
  \State $(s,r)\gets$ \Call{Readout}{$f$} \Comment{one read per view}
  \State \textbf{if} $s=\bot$ \textbf{continue}; \textbf{if} $d\notin\{\bot,s\}$ \textbf{break}
  \State $P\gets P\cdot q^{s}_f(r)$; $d\gets s$
  \State \textbf{if} $s=$ abs $\wedge P\le\tau_a$: \Call{VerifyForeground}{}; \Return \Call{Retry}{$e$}
  \State \textbf{if} $s=$ com $\wedge P\le\tau_c$: \Return \Call{Mark}{$e$, \textsf{COMMITTED}}
\EndFor
\State \Return \Call{Ask}{$e$} \Comment{disagreement or not certifiable}
\end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:recover} applies \S\ref{sec:theory} to one effect. \textsc{Readout} reads each view of a family once and returns the majority side and its count.
The thresholds come from enumerating stopping assignments (at most $4^4$ per app, precomputed) or, for joint certification, from the fixed-sequence test. \textsc{CertifiedWait} returns
the smallest logged commit-to-visibility time of the effect's fault class (the maximum over classes if unknown) whose CP upper bound on the exceedance probability is at most
its argument; a logged effect that never became visible counts as an exceedance at every wait. \textsc{Retry} reuses the nonce. Four details matter in practice.

\para{Hop 1 and monitoring.} A hop-1 verdict starts the product on either side, so the ``committed'' side of a G2 or local app is certified like any other declaration
(its bound enters $B_{\mathrm{com}}$), and a hop-1 ``absent'' verdict relaxes its own wait $w_1$, with half of the lag share (Eq.~\eqref{eq:lagsplit}). For a G2 app with an outbox entry,
\textsc{Monitor} marks the record \textsf{PENDING} and lets the agent continue; the controller re-reads hop~1 in the background until a terminal state. Its reads are fallible, so an
``acknowledged'' entry whose hop-1 bound alone does not certify is corroborated by the end-to-end families, read in the background once the entry is acknowledged (and visible).
A failed entry, or one pending longer than a stuck limit (120\,s) or close to the server's dedup horizon $D$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:twohop}), goes to the end-to-end path; there the wait is still measured from $t_0$, with $w_0$ certified from the
unconditional lag table, which is what the proof of Eq.~\eqref{eq:lagsplit} needs. Two entries mean a duplicate, which is compensated or asked about.

\para{Waiting.} The wait is the price of the lag share (Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(a,b)). If the certified wait exceeds $W_{\max}$ (120\,s), or recovery was delayed while the device
dozed and no lag table exists for that state, the planner asks instead of waiting. Local-only apps have no hop~2, so their lag share is returned to the test share. A ``committed''
reading needs no wait (A1), so a variant could read before waiting and declare early, at the cost of splitting $\beta_{\mathrm{test}}$ between the two reads of each view.

\para{Asking and security.} The prompt names the effect, shows the pre-tap screenshot and the digest, and offers ``done'', ``not done'' and ``not sure''. An app that wins
the foreground can redirect a retry~\cite{qian2026rebinding}, so \textsc{VerifyForeground} checks package, activity and window before every retry, compensation or prompt.

\para{Saga layer.} A workflow's budget is split across effects by~\eqref{eq:kkt} with each step's strongest family as its probe; a duplicate of a reversible effect detected
later (two nonce matches, or two hop-1 entries) triggers the compensating effect as a new ledger record.
