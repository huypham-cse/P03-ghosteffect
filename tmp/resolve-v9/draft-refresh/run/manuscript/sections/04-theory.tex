\section{Theory}
\label{sec:theory}

We first state what classical sequential analysis says in this model (\S\ref{sec:theory-main}), then give the reductions the agent setting needs:
certification over views (\S\ref{sec:theory-corr}), calibration (\S\ref{sec:theory-cal}), and mobile structure (\S\ref{sec:twohop}).
Full proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\subsection{The price of a budget, and two floors}
\label{sec:theory-main}
For a family of conditionally i.i.d.\ views with full support, let $u^\star=\max_y\log\frac{p_1(y)}{p_0(y)}$ and $v^\star=\max_y\log\frac{p_0(y)}{p_1(y)}$, and let
$d(x\|y)=x\log\frac{x}{y}+(1-x)\log\frac{1-x}{1-y}$. A policy is \emph{verification-only} if it ends by retrying or declaring.

\begin{theorem}[Budgeted exactly-once under a noisy, lagged oracle]
\label{thm:main}
Consider an effect with prior $\pi\in(0,1)$, lag tail $G$, and a family of conditionally i.i.d.\ views with laws $p_0,p_1$.
\begin{enumerate}[leftmargin=*,label=(\alph*),topsep=1pt,itemsep=1pt]
\item \emph{Achievability.} If $G(w)<\alpha$, waiting until $t_0+w$ and then running the SPRT on $L_n=\sum_{i\le n}\log\frac{p_1(Y_i)}{p_0(Y_i)}$, which declares when
$L_n\ge a=\log\frac1\beta$ and retries when $L_n\le-b=-\log\frac{1}{\alpha-G(w)}$, meets $(\alpha,\beta)$. Given visibility,
\begin{equation}
\E_1[N]\le\frac{a+u^\star}{D_1},\qquad \E_0[N]\le\frac{b+v^\star}{D_0}.
\label{eq:ub}
\end{equation}
\item \emph{Lag converse.} Every policy that decides by $t_0+w$ from end-to-end reads alone has $\PP(\text{retry}\mid X{=}1)\ge G(w)\,\PP(\text{retry}\mid X{=}0)$;
a verification-only one with $\PP(\text{declare}\mid X{=}0)\le\beta$ thus has $\PP(\text{retry}\mid X{=}1)\ge(1-\beta)\,G(w)$.
\item \emph{Information converse.} Every verification-only policy meeting $(\alpha,\beta)$ with $\alpha+\beta<1$ and $\E_x[N]<\infty$ satisfies the right inequality
below for any lag, and the left one if all reads follow visibility:
\begin{equation}
\E_1[N]\ge\frac{d(1-\alpha\,\|\,\beta)}{D_1},\qquad \E_0[N]\ge\frac{d(1-\beta\,\|\,\alpha)}{D_0}.
\label{eq:lb}
\end{equation}
\item \emph{Weak evidence.} Every policy that does not ask has $\PP(\dup)+\PP(\loss)\ge\E[\min(\pi_{\mathcal F},1-\pi_{\mathcal F})]$, where $\pi_{\mathcal F}$ is the posterior
of $X{=}1$ when it decides; the Bayes rule attains this. Under any lag, the right side is at least $\min(\pi,1-\pi)\big(1-\sqrt{D_0\E_0[N]/2}\big)$.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\para{What is classical.} Part~(a) is Wald's change-of-measure bound~\cite{wald1945sprt} plus a union bound on the lag; (b) is the noisy-read, finite-wait form of the
waiting argument in Li's concurrent impossibility result~\cite{li2026exactlyonce}; (c) is Lemma~1 of Kaufmann, Capp\'e and Garivier~\cite{kaufmann2016complexity}
(see also~\cite{tartakovsky2014sequential}); (d) is the binary Bayes risk with Le Cam's two-point method~\cite{tsybakov2009nonparametric}. We claim none of these inequalities
(proofs in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}).

\begin{corollary}
\label{cor:notfree}
If $0<\epsilon_1<1$, or if $G(t)>0$ for every $t$, every verification-only policy with $\PP(\text{retry}\mid X{=}0)>0$ has $\PP(\text{retry}\mid X{=}1)>0$: $\alpha=0$ forces $\beta=1$,
for unbounded stopping times too. For $\alpha,\beta>0$ with $\alpha+\beta<1$, $(\alpha,\beta)$ is reachable by verification if and only if $D_0>0$.
\end{corollary}

\para{Consequences.} Exactly-once by verification is a limit, not a point, so \sys exposes $(\alpha,\beta)$ as a knob; reads cost $\approx\log(1/\alpha)/D_0$, and the gap
between~\eqref{eq:ub} and~\eqref{eq:lb} under $X{=}1$ tends to $u^\star/D_1$ as $\alpha,\beta\to0$ provided $\alpha\log(1/\beta)\to0$. By~(b), asking or a lag-free family such as
the app's outbox is the only escape from the lag floor; otherwise the lag share is paid in waiting time, seconds for light tails and minutes to hours for Pareto tails
(Table~\ref{tab:lag}), so the planner asks when the certified wait exceeds a cap. By~(d), as $D_0\to0$ (a look-alike screen) the error approaches the prior floor for any
read budget. A fallible user makes asking cost $c_{\mathrm{ask}}+\epsilon_u[\pi_{\mathcal F}c_{\dup}+(1-\pi_{\mathcal F})c_{\loss}]$.

\subsection{Correlated reads: certify views, not reads}
\label{sec:theory-corr}
Theorem~\ref{thm:main} counts every read as fresh evidence. A VLM re-reading a screenshot does not supply that, and neither does an OCR pass over a nonce
field that is hidden. \alg therefore reads each view once and certifies \emph{family verdicts}. A family $f$ with $V_f$ views returns the majority side
$s\in\{\mathrm{abs},\mathrm{com}\}$ and the number $r$ of views on that side, or nothing on a tie. Let $q^{\mathrm{abs}}_f(r)$ bound the probability that at least $r$
views of $f$ read absent when $X{=}1$ and the effect is visible, and $q^{\mathrm{com}}_f(r)$ the probability that at least $r$ read committed when $X{=}0$.

\begin{proposition}[Family-level certification]
\label{prop:family}
Let the planner consult families in a fixed order, multiply the bounds of the verdicts seen so far, retry at the first point where the product of
$q^{\mathrm{abs}}$ terms is at most $\tau$, and ask on a contradiction. Under A2,
\[
\PP(\text{retry}\mid X{=}1,\text{visible})\;\le\;B(\tau)=\sum_{A\in\mathcal R(\tau)}\;\prod_{(f,r)\in A}q^{\mathrm{abs}}_f(r),
\]
where $\mathcal R(\tau)$ is the set of ordered assignments $A$ (each family skipped or at a level $r$) with $\prod_A q\le\tau$ and $\prod_{A\setminus\mathrm{last}(A)}q>\tau$.
The same holds for declaring with $q^{\mathrm{com}}$. No assumption is made about dependence among the views of a family.
\end{proposition}
(Proof in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}: every retry stops at an assignment in $\mathcal R(\tau)$; A2 and a union bound finish.)
The planner picks the largest $\tau$ with $B(\tau)\le\alpha_{\mathrm{test}}$. The lag adds two terms. Let $w_1$ be the wait after a hop-1 ``absent'' verdict and $w_0$ the
wait, measured from $t_0$, on every other path (hop~1 tied or absent from the app, a non-G2 entry, a failed or stuck entry). Then
\begin{equation}
\PP(\text{retry}\mid X{=}1)\le B(\tau)+q_h\,G(w_1)+G(w_0),
\label{eq:lagsplit}
\end{equation}
where $q_h$ bounds the hop-1 ``absent'' verdict. The first lag term uses only that hop-1 reads the local outbox, independent of $T$ given $X$; the second needs no
independence, because the remaining paths are disjoint and each reads end-to-end views no earlier than $t_0+w_0$. \alg certifies each term at $\alpha_{\mathrm{lag}}/2$ (all of
$\alpha_{\mathrm{lag}}$ to $w_0$ if the app has no hop-1 family).

\para{Without A2.} A2 lets bounds multiply, and it is doubtful when families read the same screens: a stale history after a restart fools nonce, digest and VLM at
once~\cite{zheng2026vpcontrol}. The remedy is to calibrate larger units.

\begin{proposition}[Joint certification]
\label{prop:joint}
Fix a score that orders verdict tuples (products of bounds from a pilot split) and let $R_\tau$ be the set of tuples on which the cascade retries at threshold $\tau$, united
over the configurations the planner runs (with or without hop~1 and the VLM). Test $\tau_1<\tau_2<\cdots$ in order on $n$ fresh calibration effects and keep the largest
$\tau_j$ such that, for all $i\le j$, the one-sided $(1-\delta)$ CP bound on $\PP(R_{\tau_i}\mid X{=}1)$ is at most $\alpha_{\mathrm{test}}$. With probability at least $1-\delta$,
$\PP(\text{retry}\mid X{=}1,\text{visible})\le\alpha_{\mathrm{test}}$, whatever the dependence across families. Certifying needs about $n\ge\ln(1/\delta)/\alpha_{\mathrm{test}}$ effects.
\end{proposition}
The regions are nested, so the guarantee fails only if the CP bound of the first $\tau$ whose true risk exceeds $\alpha_{\mathrm{test}}$ undercovers (fixed-sequence testing).
An intermediate choice keeps A2 only between \emph{evidence sources}: hop~1 reads the app's local outbox, the other three read the server-rendered history. It is available
only when hop~1 is read from a store distinct from the rendered history, which we check per app by testing hop-1 readings against $T$ on fault-injected trials (Element fails
the check). Merging the history readers into one jointly calibrated family gives $B(\tau)=q_h\,Q(\tau/q_h)+Q(\tau)$, with $Q(t)$ a CP bound on the history family reaching
product $t$, $Q(t)=0$ below the smallest reachable product and $Q(t)=1$ for $t\ge1$ (hop~1 alone then retries, whatever the history shows). Neither variant can multiply bounds of history readers, so a history-only decision again needs $n\ge\ln(1/\delta)/\alpha_{\mathrm{test}}$ effects
(\S\ref{sec:eval-pop}). How small can a family's bound be?

\begin{lemma}[Persistent-error floor]
\label{lem:floor}
Let the $V$ views of a family err conditionally i.i.d.\ given a latent per-effect error rate $\theta$ with $\E\theta=\epsilon$ and $\mathrm{Var}\,\theta=\rho\epsilon(1-\epsilon)$,
so that two views' errors have correlation $\rho$. Then
\begin{gather*}
\sup\PP(\text{all $V$ views wrong})=w+(1-w)\big(\epsilon(1-\rho)\big)^V,\\
w=\rho\epsilon/(1-\epsilon+\rho\epsilon),
\end{gather*}
attained when the family is \emph{blind} (all views wrong) with probability $w$ and otherwise errs independently at rate $\epsilon(1-\rho)$.
If the views are only exchangeable, with $m_2=\epsilon^2+\rho\epsilon(1-\epsilon)$, the supremum is $m_2$ for $2\le V\le(1-m_2)/(\epsilon-m_2)$ and strictly smaller for larger $V$.
\end{lemma}
As $V\to\infty$ the supremum tends to $w>0$: more views of one family cannot certify a side below its persistent mass (a hidden nonce field is such a blind state, $w\ge\eta$,
so certifying ``absent'' at $\alpha<\eta$ needs a second family). Within-view correlation does not matter, because no view is read twice.

\subsection{Calibration}
\label{sec:theory-cal}
\begin{lemma}[Conservative calibration]
\label{lem:calib}
(i)~For a family of i.i.d.\ binary views, running the SPRT with $\tilde\epsilon_0\ge\epsilon_0$, $\tilde\epsilon_1\ge\epsilon_1$ ($\tilde\epsilon_0+\tilde\epsilon_1<1$) preserves the
$(\alpha,\beta)$ guarantee.
(ii)~Suppose each of the $K$ bounds an effect's decision can use (verdict levels or regions, $q_h$, and the two waits) is a one-sided $(1-\delta)$ Clopper--Pearson
bound~\cite{clopper1934use} from calibration effects that are i.i.d.\ draws from the population of \emph{interrupted} deployment effects \emph{of the effect's observable fault
class} (agent death, app death seen as a pid change, connectivity loss, both), and an effect of unknown class uses the maximum over classes. Then with probability
at least $1-K\delta$ all hold; marginally, $\PP(\text{retry}\mid X{=}1)\le\alpha_{\mathrm{test}}+\alpha_{\mathrm{lag}}+K\delta$ and $\PP(\text{declare}\mid X{=}0)\le\beta_{\mathrm{test}}+K\delta$.
(iii)~If deployed family rates exceed their bounds by factors $\kappa_f\ge1$, the test term of~\eqref{eq:lagsplit} is at most $\max_{A\in\mathcal R(\tau)}\prod_{f\in A}\kappa_f\cdot B(\tau)$.
\end{lemma}
Part~(i) is a monotone coupling, (ii) a union bound, and (iii) holds term by term. Keying by class makes the lab's fault mix irrelevant (only within-class exchangeability is
needed) at the price of labels per class; the unknown-class fallback (per-family maximum, the largest wait, or for joint regions acting only when every class certifies)
uses all classes' bounds, so $K$ is multiplied by the number of classes. \sys sets $\delta=\min(\alpha,\beta)/(10K)$, with $K{=}11$ for product certification (five ``absent'' and four ``committed''
verdict levels, two waits; the nonce's false-match bound is analytic) and $K{=}5$ for joint certification, so $K\delta\le\min(\alpha_{\mathrm{cal}},\beta_{\mathrm{cal}})$. The population assumption is the one that bites:
labels from uninterrupted effects miss cached history after a kill, optimistic ``sending'' entries and blackout lags. Part~(iii) is a margin against drift that is \emph{independent}
across families; common-mode drift is what joint certification is for (\S\ref{sec:eval-pop}).

\subsection{Mobile structure: nonces and two hops}
\label{sec:twohop}
\begin{lemma}[Content-carried nonce]
\label{lem:nonce}
Suppose the effect has a free-text field that the app stores verbatim and renders in sent/history views, and that the probe reads only those views, only outgoing items of
the verified account, and never drafts, failed entries, notifications or keyboard suggestions, so an uncommitted nonce is never visible to it. The planner writes $k$ symbols
drawn uniformly from an alphabet of size $m$ and reuses them on retry. A view matches if one of its $M$ visible tokens is within edit distance $t$ of the nonce after OCR in which
each symbol errs independently with probability $e_c$. If the field is hidden, truncated or mis-tokenized (split, merged, wrapped) with probability $\eta$, then per view
\begin{equation}
\epsilon_1\le\eta+\PP[\mathrm{Bin}(k,e_c)>t],\qquad \epsilon_0\le M\,|B_t|/m^k,
\label{eq:nonce}
\end{equation}
where $|B_t|$ bounds the number of length-$k$ strings within edit distance $t$ of any token ($|B_0|=1$, $|B_1|\le km$), regardless of look-alike effects on screen.
\end{lemma}
The proof is a union bound; $|B_1|\le km$ counts substitutions, insertions into a shorter token, and deletions from a longer one. The hidden-field term $\eta$ is a blind state in the
sense of Lemma~\ref{lem:floor}, common to all nonce views. Reusing the nonce on retry also makes a later duplicate \emph{detectable}, because two items carry the same tag.

\begin{theorem}[Two-hop decomposition]
\label{thm:twohop}
Let hop~1 create $n_1$ entries in the app's local outbox and hop~2 transmit entries to the server. Assume (H1) the server is reached only through the outbox; (H2) each entry
has an app-assigned id, unique per entry, persisted with it and reused by every retransmission from the same device, also after restarts; and (H3) admissible fault schedules
have finitely many agent kills, app kills that do not cancel the app's scheduled work, transmissions lost before the server applies them, and acknowledgements lost after;
every entry is transmitted at least once on the fault-free schedule, a positive window separates an entry's creation from its first transmission, and every retransmission
happens within the server's \emph{dedup horizon} $D$ of the first application.
Let \textbf{G2} mean: entries are durable, each is retransmitted until acknowledged, and the server applies each (device, id) at most once within $D$. Then $C_e=1$ under every admissible
schedule if and only if $n_1=1$ and G2 holds. If retransmission may instead stop in a failed state shown by the app (\textbf{G2$'$}), then $n_1=1$ implies $C_e\le1$ under
every admissible schedule, and $C_e=0$ only if the entry ends failed.
\end{theorem}
($\Leftarrow$) is at-least-once delivery plus idempotent application~\cite{helland2012idempotence,birrell1984rpc,wang2021kafka}. What the agent gains is that hop~1 is
\emph{observable on the device} within seconds, so for a G2 app the late-commit regime of Theorem~\ref{thm:main}(b) becomes a local check. The planner therefore counts hop-1
entries created after $t_0$ (two entries mean a duplicate), treats ``absent'' as absent from both outbox and sent list, and monitors a pending entry until it is acknowledged,
escalating it before its age approaches $D$. ``Retrying is safe'' thus means the \emph{app's own} retry, on the same device, within $D$: an agent that redoes the step
through the GUI creates a new entry with a new id ($n_1{=}2$), which no server merges, and a re-login creates a new device. Synapse keeps transaction ids per device in a
30--60\,min cache and a 24\,h table~\cite{synapse_txn,matrix_spec}; we measured both scopes (\S\ref{sec:eval-real}). A failed entry is ambiguous end to end, because the server
may have applied it before the app gave up, so it goes to the end-to-end path; K-9~Mail enters this state by design after every lost SMTP reply, the client-side face of
the 1988 SMTP duplicate problem~\cite{rfc1047}. Force-stop cancels scheduled work and violates H3, so a force-stopped app is treated as non-G2 until its entries reach a
terminal state (Element and DAVx5 nevertheless resolved every force-stop by relaunch). Protocols differ: Matrix clients send with a client-chosen transaction id that makes
retransmission idempotent~\cite{matrix_spec}; CalDAV creates resources at a client-chosen URL with a conditional \texttt{PUT}~\cite{rfc4791,rfc9110}; SMTP has no end-to-end
dedup~\cite{rfc5321}.

\para{Budgets across a saga.} Per-step guarantees compose by a union bound under any dependence, so a workflow splits its budget, $\sum_i\alpha_i\le\alpha$, by the KKT
split of Proposition~\ref{prop:saga} (appendix); giving every step the full $\alpha$ multiplies the duplicate probability by up to $k$. An ambiguous compensation is asked about.
